# The Know-It-Alls 
The Know-it-Alls is an interactive sculpture I made for the Mallya Aditi International School's Math-Tech Fest (an interschool hackathon with ~120 participants) in the 2023-34 school year. Apart from designing and programming the sculpture, I also served as the event-day head for the Code Gauntlet event, where we created questions and prompts for ~40 students on topics such as cyphers, coding, and mathematics. Organizing this hackathon was a joint effort by my fellow Computer Science classmates, so it felt fitting to make a sculpture with motorized robots inspired by our dynamic in the classroom. You can find all of the documentation/resources in this repository!

I developed a speech-to-text/text-to-speech system to ask questions to the sculpture and generate verbal responses via ChatGPT. The motorized robots would raise their hand based on when the user's response time would end, and I used an Arduino Uno to program this function. The robots and the backdrop took me ~1.5 months to create, since everything was crafted by hand, and with the help of the teacher(s) in charge of the woodworking studio. A jig, handsaw and sandpaper sheets were used to cut the wooden blocks into their planned dimensions, and I chiseled out the facial features and expressions. Accessories came in the form of old hairpins, nuts and bolts, ~0.13" crafting wire, broken keys, gears and motherboards. The backdrop was a foam/sunboard sheet painted black, with a picture of a cityscape, which was then layered with parts of old motherboards. To create visual interest, I placed the robots at different heights and painted details-- taking inspiration from the color palettes of Piet Mondrian and a childhood TV show, CBeebies' "Little Robots".

To help people understand how to interact with the sculpture, I made a handwritten guide, and also made a counter system, which tracked that my sculpture was used ~300 times that day! This project was among the many that helped realize that my interests lay more in Electrical Engineering than Mechanical, and has helped me see that it's possible to stay in touch with my creative side while pursuing such a technical field.
